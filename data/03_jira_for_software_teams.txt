Jira Review 2025: Still the Gold Standard for Software Development Teams

Jira, built by Atlassian, has been the dominant project management tool for software development teams for over two decades. While newer tools like Asana, Monday.com, and ClickUp have gained market share, Jira remains the go-to choice for teams that follow Agile methodologies like Scrum and Kanban.

Core Strengths

Jira excels at issue tracking and sprint management. It offers purpose-built features for Agile teams: backlog grooming, sprint planning, story points, velocity charts, burndown charts, and release tracking. These are not add-ons or plugins — they are core to the product.

For development teams, Jira's deep integration with development tools is unmatched. It connects natively with Bitbucket, GitHub, and GitLab, allowing teams to link code commits, pull requests, and deployments directly to Jira issues. This traceability from requirement to deployment is something most competing tools cannot replicate.

Where Jira Falls Short

Jira's biggest weakness is its complexity. The tool is highly configurable, which means it can do almost anything — but it also means it takes significant time to set up correctly. New users often feel overwhelmed by the number of options, fields, and workflows.

Non-technical teams (marketing, HR, operations) generally find Jira too complex for their needs. Tools like Asana or Monday.com offer a much gentler learning curve for these audiences. Reviewers consistently note that Jira is "built by engineers, for engineers."

Jira's interface, while improved in recent years, still feels more utilitarian than the polished, colorful interfaces of Monday.com or ClickUp. For teams that value aesthetics and ease of use, this can be a real drawback.

Pricing

Jira offers a free tier for up to 10 users, which includes basic Scrum and Kanban boards, backlog, and reporting. The Standard plan costs $7.75 per user per month and adds advanced permissions, audit logs, and 250 GB storage. The Premium plan at $15.25 per user per month includes advanced roadmaps, sandbox environments, and IP allowlisting. Enterprise pricing is custom and includes unlimited sites, cross-organization insights, and Atlassian Intelligence.

Compared to Asana ($10.99–$24.99 per user per month) and Monday.com ($9–$19 per seat per month), Jira's pricing is competitive, especially for the Standard tier.

Jira vs Asana

Asana is better for cross-functional project management — marketing campaigns, product launches, company-wide initiatives. Jira is better for software development workflows. Many large organizations use both: Jira for their engineering teams and Asana for their business teams.

Jira vs Trello

Interestingly, Atlassian owns both Jira and Trello. Trello is the simpler, visual tool for general project management. Jira is the powerful, configurable tool for software teams. Atlassian positions them as complementary rather than competing products.

Who Should Choose Jira?

Jira is the right choice for software development teams that follow Agile methodologies and need deep integration with their development pipeline. If your team runs sprints, tracks story points, and needs visibility from backlog to deployment, Jira is purpose-built for you.

Teams that do not follow Agile, or that need a simpler tool for non-technical project management, should look elsewhere — Asana, Monday.com, or Trello will likely serve them better.
