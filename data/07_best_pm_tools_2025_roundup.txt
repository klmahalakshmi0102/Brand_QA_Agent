The 6 Best Project Management Tools in 2025: A Comprehensive Roundup

We tested and compared the six most popular project management tools to help teams make an informed decision. Here is our ranking, along with key findings for each tool.

1. Asana — Best for Growing Teams

Asana earns the top spot for its balance of power and usability. It handles complex projects with dependencies, timelines, and cross-project portfolios while remaining approachable for non-technical users. The recent addition of AI-powered status updates and smart workflows has made it even more efficient.

Standout features: Portfolio management, goal tracking, workload balancing, timeline view, forms, rules engine. Price: Free for up to 10 users; Premium at $10.99/user/month. Best for: Marketing teams, product teams, and organizations managing multiple interconnected projects.

2. Monday.com — Best for Visual Teams

Monday.com impresses with its colorful, customizable interface and powerful automation engine. It is the most visually appealing PM tool we tested and offers remarkable flexibility in how boards and workflows are configured.

Standout features: Highly customizable boards, 200+ integrations, strong automations, multiple views. Price: Free for up to 2 users; Basic at $9/seat/month (3-seat minimum). Best for: Creative agencies, marketing teams, and mid-sized organizations.

3. ClickUp — Best Value

ClickUp offers the most features per dollar of any tool in this category. Its free tier is remarkably generous, and even its paid plans undercut the competition while offering more built-in functionality. The learning curve is steeper, but the payoff is substantial.

Standout features: Built-in time tracking, docs, whiteboards, goals, and sprints. Price: Free tier with unlimited tasks; Unlimited at $7/member/month. Best for: Startups, agencies, and teams that want everything in one tool.

4. Jira — Best for Development Teams

Jira remains the undisputed champion for software development teams using Agile methodologies. Its sprint planning, backlog management, and development tool integrations are unmatched. However, it is not recommended for non-technical teams.

Standout features: Sprint planning, burndown charts, deep Git integration, advanced workflows. Price: Free for up to 10 users; Standard at $7.75/user/month. Best for: Software engineering teams, DevOps teams, and organizations following Scrum or Kanban.

5. Trello — Best for Simplicity

Trello's Kanban boards remain the easiest way to get started with project management. It is the tool we recommend for teams that have never used PM software before. Limited in advanced features, but unbeatable in simplicity.

Standout features: Intuitive Kanban boards, fast setup, generous free tier, Butler automation. Price: Free; Standard at $5/user/month. Best for: Small teams, freelancers, and simple workflows.

6. Notion — Best for Documentation-Heavy Teams

Notion blurs the line between project management and knowledge management. It is the best choice for teams that want their project tasks, documentation, and wiki in one place. Not as strong as dedicated PM tools for complex project tracking.

Standout features: Flexible block-based editor, combined docs and databases, Notion AI, wiki functionality. Price: Free for individuals; Plus at $10/user/month. Best for: Startups, content teams, and teams that value documentation alongside task management.

Key Takeaways

For most teams, Asana or Monday.com will be the right choice — they offer the best balance of features, usability, and scalability. ClickUp is the budget pick with surprising depth. Jira is essential for dev teams but overkill for everyone else. Trello is perfect for keeping things simple. Notion shines when documentation matters as much as task tracking.

No single tool is best for everyone. The right choice depends on your team size, workflow complexity, budget, and whether you need specialized features like sprint planning or document management.
