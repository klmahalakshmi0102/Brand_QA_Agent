Enterprise Project Management Tools Compared: Asana, Monday.com, and Jira at Scale

When organizations grow beyond 100 employees, project management tool selection becomes a strategic decision. The tool must handle complex permissions, cross-team visibility, compliance requirements, and integration with enterprise systems. We compare how Asana, Monday.com, and Jira perform at enterprise scale.

Security and Compliance

All three tools offer enterprise-grade security features, but with different emphasis.

Asana Enterprise includes SAML SSO, SCIM provisioning, data deletion API, organization-level admin controls, and compliance with SOC 2 Type II, GDPR, and CCPA. Asana also offers data residency options for EU customers and custom branding.

Monday.com Enterprise provides SAML SSO, SCIM, audit logs, session management, private boards, and compliance with SOC 2 Type II, GDPR, and HIPAA. The HIPAA compliance is notable — it makes Monday.com suitable for healthcare organizations, a market segment where Asana and ClickUp are not certified.

Jira Enterprise (part of Atlassian's Cloud Enterprise plan) offers SAML SSO, SCIM, organization-level audit logs, IP allowlisting, data residency, and compliance with SOC 2, ISO 27001, and FedRAMP. Atlassian's security infrastructure is the most mature of the three, which matters for regulated industries.

Cross-Team Visibility

Asana excels here with its portfolio and goal features. Executives can see all projects across the organization, their status, and how they align to company goals — without needing to open individual projects. The workload view shows resource allocation across teams, helping leaders identify bottlenecks.

Monday.com offers cross-board dashboards that aggregate data from multiple boards. While visually impressive, the setup requires more manual configuration than Asana's portfolio approach. Monday.com's recently added Goals feature is catching up but is not yet as mature as Asana's.

Jira's Advanced Roadmaps (Premium and Enterprise) provide cross-team planning for software organizations. It maps dependencies across teams and sprints, which is powerful for engineering organizations but less useful for non-technical departments.

Enterprise Pricing

Enterprise pricing for all three tools is custom and negotiated. General ranges based on public information and user reports:

Asana Enterprise: Approximately $30–35 per user per month for large deployments.
Monday.com Enterprise: Approximately $25–30 per seat per month for large deployments.
Jira Enterprise: Part of Atlassian Cloud Enterprise at approximately $15–20 per user per month (includes Jira and Confluence).

Jira's pricing advantage at enterprise scale is significant, especially since Confluence (wiki/docs) is bundled. However, if the organization needs PM capabilities beyond software development, they may end up paying for Asana or Monday.com in addition to Jira.

Integration with Enterprise Systems

All three integrate with major enterprise tools (Salesforce, Microsoft 365, Google Workspace, Slack, Teams). Asana and Monday.com additionally integrate with business intelligence tools like Tableau and Power BI, which is valuable for executive reporting.

Jira's integration advantage is in the development toolchain: Bitbucket, GitHub, GitLab, Jenkins, and the broader Atlassian ecosystem (Confluence, Statuspage, Opsgenie). For engineering-centric organizations, this ecosystem is hard to match.

Recommendation for Enterprise Buyers

Multi-department organizations (engineering, marketing, sales, operations all need PM): Asana provides the best cross-functional experience with portfolios, goals, and workload management.

Engineering-heavy organizations: Jira plus Confluence offers the best value and deepest development integration. Non-engineering teams can use Jira's simplified business projects or a separate tool.

Visual and creative organizations (agencies, media, marketing): Monday.com's visual interface and HIPAA compliance make it the strongest choice for industries where data compliance matters and teams are less technical.

Many enterprise organizations end up using two tools — Jira for engineering and either Asana or Monday.com for business teams. While not ideal, this pragmatic approach plays to each tool's strengths.
