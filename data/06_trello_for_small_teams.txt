Trello Review 2025: The Simple Kanban Board That Still Works for Millions

In a market filled with increasingly complex project management tools, Trello remains refreshingly simple. Owned by Atlassian since 2017, Trello sticks to what it does best: visual Kanban boards that anyone can use within minutes.

Why Trello Endures

Trello's core value proposition has not changed since its launch in 2011: boards, lists, and cards. You create a board for a project, add lists for stages (To Do, Doing, Done), and create cards for tasks. Drag cards between lists as work progresses. That is it.

This simplicity is deliberate. While competitors like ClickUp and Monday.com add feature after feature, Trello resists bloat. The result is a tool that is almost universally easy to learn. Reviewers consistently rate Trello highest for ease of use across all major PM tools.

Trello's Kanban Approach

Trello is a Kanban tool through and through. If your team thinks in terms of visual boards and flowing work, Trello fits naturally. Each card can hold descriptions, checklists, due dates, attachments, labels, and comments. Power-Ups (plugins) add additional functionality like calendar views, voting, and integrations.

What Trello does not offer natively: Gantt charts, timeline views, resource management, sprint planning, or advanced reporting. These are available through Power-Ups and third-party integrations, but they are not part of the core experience.

Trello Pricing in Detail

Trello's free tier is solid for small teams. It includes unlimited cards, up to 10 boards per workspace, unlimited Power-Ups per board, and 250 workspace command runs per month for automation.

The Standard plan at $5 per user per month adds unlimited boards, advanced checklists, custom fields, and 1,000 workspace command runs. The Premium plan at $10 per user per month includes timeline view, dashboard view, calendar view, and workspace table view — essentially adding the views that competing tools offer natively. The Enterprise plan starts at $17.50 per user per month with organization-wide permissions and unlimited workspaces.

Even at Premium pricing, Trello remains cheaper than most competitors. Asana Premium is $10.99, Monday.com Standard is $12, and ClickUp Business is $12.

Who Still Uses Trello?

Trello has over 50 million registered users and remains popular with freelancers, small teams, and non-technical departments within larger organizations. Common use cases include content calendars, editorial workflows, bug tracking, personal productivity, event planning, and CRM-style deal tracking.

Larger teams and complex projects often outgrow Trello. When a team needs Gantt charts, workload management, or cross-project reporting, they typically migrate to Asana, Monday.com, or ClickUp.

Trello vs Asana for Small Teams

For teams under 10 people with simple workflows, Trello and Asana's free tiers are both viable. Trello is faster to set up and more visual. Asana offers more structure and scales better as the team grows. The choice often comes down to personal preference: do you think in boards (Trello) or lists (Asana)?

Bottom Line

Trello is not trying to be everything to everyone. It is a simple, affordable, visual task management tool that excels for small teams and straightforward workflows. If you need more, you will outgrow it. But for millions of teams, "simple and visual" is exactly right.
