Notion Review 2025: The All-in-One Workspace That Tries to Replace Everything

Notion is not a traditional project management tool — it is an all-in-one workspace that combines notes, documents, databases, wikis, and project management into a single platform. This ambitious scope is both its greatest strength and its most common criticism.

What Makes Notion Different

While tools like Asana, Trello, and Jira focus specifically on task and project management, Notion aims to be the single tool where teams do all their work. You can write meeting notes, build a company wiki, track projects, manage sprints, and create custom databases — all within Notion.

The building block of Notion is the "block." Everything is a block: text paragraphs, headings, to-do items, images, embeds, databases, and more. You assemble pages from blocks, and those pages can be nested infinitely. This gives Notion extraordinary flexibility but also means there is no single "right way" to use it.

Project Management Capabilities

Notion's project management features are built on its database system. You create a database with properties like status, assignee, due date, priority, and tags. This database can then be viewed as a table, board (Kanban), timeline, calendar, or list. In 2024, Notion added built-in Sprints support, making it more competitive with Jira for development teams.

Compared to purpose-built PM tools, Notion's project management is capable but not as specialized. Asana offers better workload management, portfolio tracking, and goal setting. Jira offers deeper Agile features. Monday.com offers more powerful automations. But Notion lets you combine project management with documentation in a way no other tool matches.

Notion AI

Notion has invested heavily in AI features. Notion AI can summarize pages, generate content, extract action items from meeting notes, translate text, and answer questions about your workspace. This AI integration is a significant differentiator — while Asana and Monday.com have added AI features, Notion's implementation is more deeply embedded in the writing and documentation experience.

Pricing

Notion offers a free plan for individuals with unlimited blocks and pages. The Plus plan at $10 per user per month adds unlimited file uploads, 30-day version history, and custom automations. The Business plan at $18 per user per month includes private team spaces, bulk export, and advanced permissions. Enterprise pricing is custom and adds SAML SSO, SCIM provisioning, and audit logs. Notion AI is an additional $10 per user per month on any plan.

Reviewers note that Notion's pricing is competitive with Asana and Monday.com, especially considering that it replaces not just a PM tool but also potentially a wiki (Confluence), a docs tool (Google Docs), and a simple database.

Notion vs Asana

Asana is a better pure project management tool. It has more structured workflows, better reporting, and purpose-built features for managing complex projects. Notion is the better choice for teams that want to combine project management with documentation, knowledge management, and internal wikis in a single platform. Many teams use both — Notion for docs and wiki, Asana for project tracking.

Notion vs Trello

Trello is simpler and faster to set up for basic Kanban workflows. Notion is far more flexible but requires more time to configure. If you just need a visual board for tasks, Trello gets you there in minutes. If you want a connected workspace where project tasks link to design docs and meeting notes, Notion is the better fit.

Who Should Choose Notion?

Notion is ideal for teams that want one tool for everything — project management, documentation, wikis, and lightweight databases. Startups, small teams, and content-heavy organizations particularly benefit from Notion's flexibility. Teams that need heavy-duty project management features (resource management, advanced reporting, Gantt dependencies) may find dedicated PM tools more suitable.
